      Four ways to make a salt
      MethodUse it whenKey points
      PrecipitationThe salt is insolubleMix two soluble salts. Filter, wash the residue with distilled water, dry.
      TitrationThe salt is soluble and both reactants are soluble (Group I or ammonium salts)Find the volumes with an indicator, repeat without it, then evaporate and crystallise.
      Acid + insoluble base or carbonateThe salt is soluble and the solid is insolubleAdd excess solid, filter it off, evaporate to saturation, cool, filter, wash, dry. Effervescence stops with carbonates.
      Acid + metalThe metal is moderately reactive (Mg, Zn, Fe)Hydrogen forms. Too violent for Na or K. Cu does not react with dilute acid.

      Purify crystals by recrystallisation: dissolve in the minimum of hot water, filter while hot, cool slowly, filter and wash with a little cold distilled water.

      Salts, oxides and structure
      Salt made fromSolution isExample
      Strong acid + strong baseNeutralNaCl, KNO₃
      Strong acid + weak baseAcidicNH₄Cl (NH₄⁺ gives H⁺)
      Weak acid + strong baseBasicCH₃COONa (CH₃COO⁻ takes H⁺)
      Weak acid + weak baseCompare Ka and KbNH₄CN is basic because Kb(CN⁻) > Ka(NH₄⁺)
      Small, highly charged metal ionAcidicAl³⁺, Fe³⁺ (hydrated ions donate H⁺)

      Oxides. Metal oxides are basic (Na₂O, MgO). Al₂O₃ is amphoteric. Non-metal oxides are acidic (SO₃, P₄O₁₀, CO₂).

      Acid strength. Down a group, H–X gets weaker so HI > HF. For oxyacids, more O atoms or a more electronegative centre means a stronger acid (HClO₄ > HClO). Electron-withdrawing groups strengthen acids (CF₃COOH > CH₃COOH).

      Lewis model. Acids accept an electron pair (H⁺, BF₃, Fe³⁺). Bases donate one (NH₃, OH⁻, H₂O).
